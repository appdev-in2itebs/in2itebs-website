## 1. The big idea — what the website is trying to say

In2IT EBS describes itself as *"an AI-led enterprise transformation company across Platforms, Advisory and Digital Engineering."* In everyday terms: it helps large organisations modernise the core business software they run on, advises them on what to change, and builds the new digital and data tools around it — all under one roof.

The single most important job of the website is to make a mid-sized firm look and feel like a **credible, single-accountable partner** — the kind of trusted name a large company would hire — without pretending to be one of the giant global consultancies. We achieve that through a **confident, magazine-quality look** rather than through sheer volume of content. Think of the polish of a high-end business publication (the kind of editorial feel you see in the Financial Times or The Economist), applied to a technology company.

**A note on one recurring phrase: "Converged Intelligence."** This is the firm's own term for its way of working — clear ownership of each business area, one shared delivery team underneath them all, and artificial intelligence (AI) built into everything rather than added as an afterthought. The website treats this as the firm's signature idea, not just one feature among many.

## 2. The overall style — and why it looks premium

The website will feel calm, confident and editorial. Five simple principles guide every page:

1. **It reads like a magazine, not a corporate template.** Most large competitors use a single plain typeface everywhere, which looks generic. We pair an elegant "storybook" headline style with a clean, modern body style. That combination signals quality and authority — and it's what the strongest competitors (IBM, Wipro and others) do too.

2. **Deep navy is the hero colour; bright blue is used like a highlighter.** Navy carries most of the visual weight on every page. The brighter accent blue appears only on a single emphasised word in a headline, on links, and on one or two key call-outs per page. This restraint is exactly what makes the look feel expensive rather than "ordinary corporate blue."

3. **Proof over promises.** The deck is full of real, named, measurable results — 25-plus major software programmes delivered, 99.5% data accuracy, brownfield migrations done in five months, and named clients such as a Telangana power utility, Wipro Infrastructure Engineering and Triveni Turbines. The competitor research found that rivals are weak on named proof. So every important page puts a real number or a named result high up, where visitors see it immediately.

4. **The firm's own tools are made visible.** In2IT EBS has several branded, in-house tools and methods that today are buried in dense text. We give each one a clear, repeatable on-screen treatment and a place in the navigation menu, so visitors can see the firm has genuine intellectual property of its own. *(Intellectual property simply means tools and methods the firm built and owns.)*

5. **Gentle, purposeful movement; fast pages.** Animation is subtle — things fade in softly as you scroll. We deliberately avoid auto-rotating slideshows, which the research found rivals overuse and visitors dislike.

**Why this beats the competition.** Against the giant consultancies, we win on editorial polish rather than scale. Against similar-sized rivals — who tend to use bland stock photos, repetitive "Read More" links and rotating banners — we win on disciplined design, purposeful wording and a consistent picture style. Against photo-heavy peers, we borrow their instinct to *prove things with imagery* but keep a more modern, AI-appropriate abstract graphic as our centrepiece.

## 4. The two fonts — and what they convey

The website uses a deliberate two-font system.

- **The headline font (a refined serif called Newsreader).** A serif is a typeface with small "feet" on the letters, the kind you see in quality books and newspapers. This font gives titles their distinctive editorial, trustworthy character. In every major headline, one key word is set in *italic* and in accent blue — this is the brand's signature move, lifted straight from the deck (for example, *"Data migration is the **real risk** in S/4HANA"*).

- **The everyday font (a clean sans-serif called IBM Plex Sans).** "Sans-serif" simply means without the little feet — a clean, modern look. It handles all the readable content: introductions, body text, captions, tables, and the small spaced-out capital-letter labels that mark each section (for example, "AT A GLANCE").

For Word documents and emails, where those two fonts may not be installed on every machine, the firm falls back to **Calibri** so documents always look correct.

Together: the serif says *established and editorial*; the sans-serif says *modern and precise*. The pairing is what gives the brand its premium, confident voice.

## 5. Three business areas — told apart, yet clearly one family

The deck organises everything into **three pillars** (the firm's word for its three main business areas), plus a shared delivery engine that sits underneath all three:

- **Pillar A — Platform Services:** modernising the big business-software platforms (SAP, Salesforce, Workday).
- **Pillar B — Advisory Services:** the consulting layer that decides *what* to change and *why*.
- **Pillar C — Digital, Data & AI Services:** the engineering layer that builds modern apps, data tools and AI.
- **(D) Delivery Excellence:** the single shared delivery team and method that powers all three.

The design's challenge is to make these instantly distinguishable while still looking like one brand. The trick: **only one thing changes between them** — a single accent colour and a simple background pattern. Everything else stays identical, which guarantees they feel like one family.

| | A — Platform Services | B — Advisory Services | C — Digital, Data & AI | D — Delivery Excellence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Letter** | A | B | C | D |
| **Accent colour** | Navy | Accent blue | Light blue | Sand (the shared one) |
| **Its nature (from the deck)** | Platform-led | Consulting-led | Engineering-led | Shared delivery engine |
| **Background pattern** ★ Additive | Stacked layered tiles | Concentric arcs, like a compass | A network of connected dots | Horizontal "track" lanes |

How each area shows itself, in three consistent ways:

1. **A small lettered badge** (A, B or C with its label) appears on each area's main page and in the navigation menu — a quiet wayfinding cue, taken from the deck's own "PILLAR A / PILLAR B" labelling.
2. **A single thin coloured line and a faint background pattern.** Each area carries one slim line in its accent colour and a large, very faint background motif behind its banner — enough to feel distinct, never decorative clutter.
3. **A coloured card edge.** Cards belonging to an area carry a thin coloured stripe down their left edge. When all four sit side by side (on the home page, for example), they read instantly as one orderly set.

**Why this stays cohesive ★ Additive:** no new colours are ever introduced — both accent blue and light blue already exist in the palette — and all four background patterns are drawn in the same simple, single-line style, so they look like one illustration family.

## 9. The motion — what gently moves, and where

## 10. Accessibility, mobile and speed — in plain terms

**Accessibility (so everyone can use it).** The site is built to a recognised accessibility standard. Text contrast is strong enough to read comfortably; small accent-blue text is deepened to be safe. Everything can be operated with a keyboard alone, not just a mouse — important for people who cannot use a pointer. Colour is never the *only* way information is conveyed (the area badges always carry a letter as well as a colour). Every photo and logo has a written description for screen-reader users, and all movement respects the "reduce motion" setting.

**Mobile (phones and tablets).** The layout is designed for small screens first. On a phone, the top menu collapses into a tidy expandable menu with the main button and region switcher kept handy; multi-column grids stack into a single readable column; the long SAP page's follow-along menu becomes a slim bar across the top; and spacing tightens so pages don't feel stretched.

**Speed.** Pages are built to load fast and feel instant. Photos are delivered in efficient, modern formats and only loaded as you scroll to them; the two fonts are hosted by the firm itself (no reliance on an outside service); a single icon set keeps the site lean; and image spaces are reserved in advance so nothing jumps around while loading. The target is a fast first view and a stable, no-jump layout.

## Glossary — plain-language definitions

- **AI (Artificial Intelligence):** software that performs tasks normally needing human judgement — such as answering questions, spotting patterns, or making recommendations.
- **AMS (Application Management Services):** the ongoing "run and support" service that keeps business software healthy, fixes issues and makes improvements after the initial project is done.
- **BFSI (Banking, Financial Services and Insurance):** a common shorthand for the finance industry as a whole.
- **Brownfield / Greenfield (migration approaches):** *Brownfield* means upgrading the existing system in place, keeping its history and customisations. *Greenfield* means starting fresh with a clean new system. (*Selective Data Transition* is a middle path — a fresh system that carries over only chosen data.)
- **Cloud:** delivering software and computing power over the internet rather than from machines on a company's own premises. The big providers are Amazon, Microsoft and Google.
- **Converged Intelligence:** In2IT EBS's own term for its operating approach — clear ownership of each business area, one shared delivery team underneath, and AI built into everything.
- **ERP (Enterprise Resource Planning):** the central business software that runs core operations — finance, procurement, manufacturing, HR and more — in one connected system.
- **GROW with SAP:** a faster, lower-cost, pre-configured version of SAP's modern system aimed at mid-sized organisations.
- **PCE (Private Cloud Edition):** a version of SAP's modern system hosted on dedicated cloud space, for organisations needing heavy customisation or stricter data isolation.
- **RISE with SAP:** SAP's all-in-one package that bundles its modern system together with cloud hosting and supporting services.
- **S/4HANA:** SAP's current-generation core business software — the modern successor to its older "ECC" system.
- **SAP:** one of the world's largest business-software companies; its products run the core operations of many large enterprises. ("SAP Gold Partner," "PCE Partner" and "Co-Innovation Partner" are levels of recognition SAP awards to expert delivery firms.)
- **SAP Joule:** SAP's built-in AI assistant that lets people ask questions and get help in plain language inside SAP software.
- **Salesforce / Workday:** two other major business-software platforms — Salesforce for customer relationships and sales; Workday for HR and finance.
- **Syniti:** a specialist data-migration toolset and method used to move information safely and accurately into a new system.
